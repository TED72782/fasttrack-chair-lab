What this does and does not know

  • Where the numbers come from. Arrival pattern, how long people occupy a space, and the half who need a test are all measured from Mary Bridge ESI 4/5 patients, 2025 onward. Nothing here is invented except the assumptions flagged in the next item.
  • Two assumptions, and both are controls rather than constants. The first is how long a patient keeps the assessment space before moving on — the slider under Timing. Nothing measures it: what we can measure is that a test patient's first order lands {{toorder}} minutes after rooming. The {{refasw}}-minute reference this page also quotes is that {{toorder}} plus an assumed {{pom}} minutes after the order — nothing timestamps when a patient could actually move, so that {{pom}} was chosen, not measured. Neither number is a decision anyone made, and neither exists at all for the half who need no test. It is the number that decides the best split, so move it and watch the split move. The second is what happens, in the combined-zone layouts, to the half of patients who need no test at all. Nothing measures that either, and it decides whether two assessment chairs can work. Try both ways.
  • The lane closes when you close it, and that is modelled. Anyone still waiting for a space goes to the main department. Without that rule the simulation quietly served its backlog in overtime — a six-space lane ran until 09:04 the next morning and spent 45% of its work outside its own hours, which made every layout's worst hour land at 22:00 and read like a demand peak. It was the overtime bucket.
  • Read the wait and the diversions together. The wait already includes the patients sent to the main department — but only for what they waited in the lane, up to closing. What they wait after that is not in it; the score is what charges that. So a layout that sends people away looks worse on the wait, not better: at its most crowded those patients are the ones waiting longest. The wait is the lane's own queue; the score is the whole evening.
  • The score prices everyone who arrives, including the ones you turn down. Every other number here improves if the lane takes fewer patients — narrow the criteria, or let the queue spill past closing, and the average among those you served looks better while the evening is unchanged. So the score charges the patients a lane does not serve what they actually get instead: today's wait for a room in the main department, by hour — {{main15}} minutes at 15:00 rising to {{main22}} at 22:00. Accept nobody and you score exactly the status quo. There is no way to win by shrinking.
  • There is no "no fast track" comparison, and there cannot be. The department has run one throughout the period measured here, so no lane-free day exists to compare against. This page briefly offered one, built by excluding the patients who went through today's pod — it was withdrawn because {{walkoutpct}}% of the walkouts in those hours have no care area recorded at all, so they fell into the "no fast track" group by bookkeeping rather than by outcome, and what remained was the pod's overflow rather than a counterfactual.
  • The bar is today, and today already has a fast track. An ESI 4/5 patient arriving between 15:00 and 23:00 waits {{mean1523}} minutes on average now. The score compares against {{mainmean}} rather than {{mean1523}}, because it prices every ESI 4/5 patient of the day and the quieter daytime hours are in that average too. Both are today; they differ only in which patients they count. But the Orca pod is already taking {{podlo}}–{{podhi}}% of them until {{poduntil}}:00, and the whole hourly escalation — {{main15}} minutes at 15:00 to {{main22}} at 22:00 — is that pod closing: its patients wait a flat {{podwlo}}–{{podwhi}} minutes at every hour, while everyone else goes {{otherlo}} to {{otherhi}}. Whether a proposed lane is added to today's pod or replaces it therefore changes what it should be compared against, and this page does not try to settle that — for the reason in the bullet above, there is no lane-free bar to offer.
  • The clock starts at the door. A patient cannot be put anywhere before registration and the start of triage — {{floor}} minutes on average here, barely varying across the evening. That is added to every lane wait, because the figure it is compared against — today's arrival-to-room — contains it too. Without it the lane was being credited with about 8 minutes it never saves.
  • It assumes the main department is unaffected. Taking work out of it should make its wait shorter, which this does not model — so if anything the score understates what excluding patients costs.
  • Whether narrowing helps depends on the layout, not on a rule. At ten spaces a lane taking everyone beats one taking only the easy complaints. At six it is the other way round, because six cannot absorb them. Do not carry either result across to a different footprint.
  • The split follows the assessment time — it is not a fixed answer. The best split at ten spaces runs 2+8 if the assessment ends at 20 minutes and 6+4 if it ends at 90, tracking how the visit divides between the two sides. What barely moves is the score you can achieve: about three minutes across that whole range, provided the split matches the time. So the question to settle is when an assessment is finished, not which split is right in the abstract.
  • There is no pass mark, on purpose. Nothing measured here says how long a patient may reasonably wait, so the page does not pretend to know. A line drawn at a number nobody can justify turns every comparison into a verdict against it. Compare the layouts with each other, and decide what is acceptable yourselves.
  • Waits are averages, and small differences are noise. Most patients wait nothing at all, and half an evening's delay can land in a single hour — which is why the worst hour is shown separately. Two layouts a minute or two apart are not reliably in that order; rerun them and they can swap.
  • It cannot see the ward. Who is watching a patient waiting on a result, whether an open chair is right for a given child, and what happens at 23:00 are all real and none of them are in here. If pooling wins on this screen and loses on the floor, the floor is right.
