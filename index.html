What this does and does not know

  • Where the numbers come from. Arrival pattern, how long people occupy a space, and the half who need a test are all measured from Mary Bridge ESI 4/5 patients, 2025 onward. Nothing here is invented except the one item below.
  • Two assumptions, and both are controls rather than constants. How long someone keeps the assessment space after their test is ordered (we assume 25 minutes — it moves the divided layouts and does not touch the pooled one), and, in the combined-zone layouts, what happens to the half of patients who need no test at all. Nothing measures the second and it decides whether two assessment chairs can work. Try it both ways.
  • When a layout is over capacity, the wait is not a real number. If the assessment chairs cannot carry the evening, the queue never clears and the wait just grows with the length of the run. The banner above says so instead of showing you a large figure.
  • Waits are averages, and close calls are coin flips. Most patients wait nothing at all. Half an evening's total delay can land in one hour, which is why the target here is the worst hour. But a layout landing within a minute or two of 15 is not reliably a pass — rerun it and it can land the other side.
  • It cannot see the ward. Who is watching a patient waiting on a result, whether an open chair is right for a given child, and what happens at 23:00 are all real and none of them are in here. If pooling wins on this screen and loses on the floor, the floor is right.
